Why a full-day, five-day program?

Many families try an hour of therapy here and there. Here's why we run a full school-style day, Monday to Friday, and why it can make a real difference for young children.

Balak Drithi Autism Care · 5-minute read

Learning needs practice, and practice needs time

Think about how any child learns to talk, ride a cycle or eat with a spoon: by doing it many times, in many places, with lots of encouragement. Children with autism learn the same way. They usually need more practice, not less.

An hour of therapy twice a week gives a child a few chances to practise a skill. A full day gives them hundreds, spread across real activities: asking for juice at snack time, waiting for a turn on the swing, washing hands before lunch.

A major report from the US National Research Council recommended at least 25 hours a week of structured support, year-round, for young children with autism.

Five reasons we chose a full day

1. Routine helps children feel safe

Many children with autism feel calmer when they know what comes next. The same arrival, the same rhythm of blocks and the same faces, every weekday, make the day predictable, and a calm child is ready to learn.

2. Skills learned one day are practised the next

When there are long gaps between sessions, children can lose what they learned. Daily practice keeps new skills fresh until they become habits.

3. Friends and group life

Short 1:1 sessions can't teach a child to play with other children. A full day includes peer play, group activities and meals together, with support.

4. Getting ready for school

Sitting for an activity, following a routine, moving between tasks and being part of a group: a full day practises the skills children need for a school classroom.

5. Everything in one place

ABA, occupational therapy and, when needed, speech and physiotherapy all happen at the center, with one team sharing one plan. No rushing between clinics in Chennai traffic.

An honest note: more hours is not automatically better. What matters most is the quality of those hours: trained therapists, a plan built for your child, and a child who is happy to take part. That's why our day includes play, art, meals and rest (younger children nap after lunch), and why we follow your child's comfort through assent-based care. A long day only helps if it's a good day.

What about parents?

A full day also gives families steady support. Parents get regular updates and simple ways to practise at home, and many find it easier to keep working or care for other children knowing their child is learning in a safe place.
