Why every child gets their own 16-block plan, with OT built in

At Balak Drithi, a child's day isn't a timetable we hand out to everyone. It is a plan built around your child, half an hour at a time.

Balak Drithi Autism Care · 5-minute read

What is a "block"?

Our day runs from 8:30 to 4:30, Monday to Friday. We divide it into 16 blocks of 30 minutes. Each block has a purpose, and each child's mix of blocks is chosen for them.

Thirty minutes is long enough to learn something properly, and short enough that young children stay engaged. Changing activity every half hour also gives natural breaks, so the day feels varied rather than tiring.

What goes into a child's day?

  • 1:1 teaching: focused time with a therapist on specific skills, such as asking for things, matching, following instructions.
  • Learning through play: the same goals practised in natural moments, like asking for a turn on the swing or naming a colour while building.
  • Art, sensory and peer play every day: creativity, movement and time with other children, with learning goals quietly built in.
  • Meals and snack time: real practice in eating, waiting, cleaning up and being part of a group.
  • Rest: children under 4 get a rest after lunch, because tired children can't learn.
  • Occupational therapy (OT) for every child: two half-hour OT sessions a week are part of every child's plan. Speech therapy and physiotherapy are added when your child's assessment shows they will help.

Why build OT into the week?

Many children with autism also have sensory needs (strong reactions to noise, touch or movement) or find fine-motor tasks like holding a crayon or buttoning a shirt hard. That is what occupational therapists help with.

When OT happens in a separate clinic, on a different day, with a different team, the goals often don't connect. When OT is part of the same plan:

  • The occupational therapist and the ABA team share goals. If OT is working on holding a pencil, the art block practises it too.
  • Sensory strategies that help your child calm down are used all day, not only in OT.
  • You don't have to drive across Chennai to another appointment after the center day.

Why "customised" matters

Two children of the same age can need very different days. One may need more 1:1 teaching to build communication; another may need more peer play to build friendships, or more rest and sensory breaks.

Your child's plan starts from their assessment and individual plan (IIP). A senior clinician (BCBA) reviews progress regularly and changes the blocks as your child grows. A block that worked in January may be swapped for something new by March.

Why do therapists rotate? Your child works with more than one therapist during the week. That is on purpose: a skill learned with only one adult often stays with that adult. Practising with different people helps children use their skills everywhere, including at home and, later, at school.

What this means for your family

  • One coordinated plan instead of many separate appointments.
  • A balanced day: focused learning, play, friends, food and rest.
  • Clear goals you can see, and simple ways to practise the same skills at home.
