What is assent-based ABA?

We ask for a child's cooperation. We never demand it. Here is what that means in a real therapy session.

Balak Drithi Autism Care · 5-minute read

Consent and assent: what's the difference?

Consent is the permission you give as a parent: you agree to therapy for your child.

Assent is your child's own agreement, moment by moment, to take part. Young children, and children who don't yet use many words, can't sign a form. But they can show us clearly whether they are happy to join in, through their faces, bodies, sounds and actions.

Assent-based ABA means the therapist keeps watching for that agreement throughout the session, and changes course when it disappears.

How do we know a child is saying "yes"?

Children say "yes, let's play" in many ways, with or without words:

  • Coming to the table or the activity on their own
  • Leaning in, reaching for the materials, starting a task themselves
  • Smiling, laughing, a relaxed face and body
  • Choosing what to do next, or saying "yes" or "again"

And how do we notice "not right now"?

When a child withdraws assent, they are communicating something important. Signs can include:

  • Turning away, walking away or leaving the area
  • Pushing or swiping materials away
  • Crying, saying "no", or covering ears or eyes
  • Becoming very still, tense or upset

In older styles of therapy, these moments were often treated as "problem behavior" to push through. In assent-based ABA, we treat them as communication. The child is telling us that something is too hard, too long, too loud, or simply not interesting right now.

What happens when a child says "not right now"?

We pause. Then we work out what changed and adjust:

  • Offer a break or a favourite activity
  • Make the task easier or shorter
  • Give more choices about what, where or how
  • Change the room, the noise or the materials

Most of the time, the child returns to learning once they feel safe and in control, and the learning sticks better because they want to be there. Over time, children also learn something powerful: their communication matters, and adults listen.

Does that mean the child decides everything? No. Safety always comes first, and some routines (like crossing the road holding hands) are not optional. Assent-based ABA is not "no structure". It is structure built around the child's comfort, so that learning feels safe instead of forced.

Why it matters

  • Trust: children learn best from adults they trust.
  • Real communication: respecting "no" teaches children that their words and signals work.
  • Safety: children who can say "stop" and be heard are better protected, now and as they grow.
  • Professional ethics: the Behavior Analyst Certification Board's Ethics Code (Standard 2.11) asks behavior analysts to seek a client's assent where applicable, alongside a parent's consent. Leading practitioners, especially in the United States, have moved towards assent-based practice, and we are bringing it to families in India.

Questions to ask any therapy center

  • "What do your therapists do when my child doesn't want to continue?"
  • "How do you know when my child is happy to take part?"
  • "How are therapists trained to notice signs of distress?"
  • "Can I watch a session?"

A good center will welcome these questions. At Balak Drithi, every therapist is trained in assent-based practice before they work with a child on their own.
