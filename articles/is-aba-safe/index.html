Is ABA therapy safe? Your questions, answered honestly

If you have searched for ABA online, you may have seen posts calling it harmful, or asking for it to be banned. You deserve a straight answer.

Balak Drithi Academy · 6-minute read

The short answer

The worry is understandable, and some of it is fair. Some ABA programs in the past, mostly decades ago and mostly abroad, had children sitting at tables for many hours, repeating drills, and were pushed to comply even when they were clearly upset. Some autistic adults who went through programs like that have spoken about how it felt, and their experiences deserve to be heard.

But that is not what good ABA looks like today, and it is not what we do. ABA is a science of how children learn. How it is used is what matters, and it is why we chose assent-based ABA.

Older ABA vs assent-based ABA

What went wrong before

  • Long hours of repetitive table drills
  • "Compliance" as the goal
  • A child's distress treated as a problem to push through
  • Trying to stop harmless behaviors like hand-flapping
  • Parents kept outside the room

What we do instead

  • Learning through play, routines and friends
  • Independence and communication as the goal
  • A child's "no" is listened to: we pause and adjust
  • Harmless stimming is left alone
  • Parents as partners, welcome to observe

What does the research say?

Research shows that ABA-based early intervention helps many young children improve communication, everyday skills and readiness for school. The research also has limits: the quality of studies varies, and results differ from child to child. We think it is important to say both. No single method is right for every child, which is why ABA sits alongside speech and occupational therapy in every child's plan.

Our promise: we never force a child, we never use punishment, and if your child says "no" we listen. You will always know what we are working on and why.

Questions parents ask us

Is ABA safe for my child?

When it is done well, by trained people, with your child's assent and your involvement, yes. The concerns people raise are real, and they come from how some ABA was practised in the past: long hours of drills, pressure to comply, and ignoring a child's distress. Assent-based ABA is built to avoid exactly that.

I've read online that ABA is harmful and should be banned. Is that true?

Some autistic adults and parents had painful experiences with older, rigid programs, and their voices matter. Many professionals agree those practices were wrong. But ABA as a whole is a science of how people learn, and modern practice has changed: it now asks for a child's assent, uses play, and respects a child's 'no'. Research shows ABA-based early intervention helps many children with communication and everyday skills, though results vary from child to child and no single method is right for everyone.

Will my child be forced or punished?

No. If your child says 'no', or shows they are upset, we pause and change what we are doing: a break, an easier task, a different activity. We do not use punishment. The only time an adult will hold a child is to keep them safe in the moment, for example near a road.

Will my child sit at a table doing drills all day?

No. Most learning happens through play, routines like meals and dressing, and time with other children. Short table activities are used only when a child enjoys them and is ready.

Will you try to stop my child from being autistic, or stop their stimming?

No. Our goal is not to make your child look 'less autistic'. It is to help them communicate, be safe and be more independent. We don't try to stop harmless stimming like hand-flapping or rocking. We only work on behaviors that hurt your child or others, or that get in the way of learning.

Is ABA the only therapy you use?

No. Speech therapy and occupational therapy are part of every child's plan, alongside ABA, so one team works towards the same goals.

Can I watch my child's sessions?

Yes. You will get daily notes, regular parent coaching, and you are welcome to observe. A center that does not let parents see how their child is taught is a warning sign.

Who will work with my child?

Every therapist completes about 40 hours of training, including assent-based practice, child safety (POCSO) and first aid, then works alongside an experienced colleague before working with a child on their own. A senior clinician reviews each child's progress regularly.

How will I know if it is working?

We set clear goals with you, like asking for water, using the toilet, or playing with another child, and we share progress with you every week. If something isn't working, we change it.

Questions to ask any center, including ours

  • "What do you do when my child doesn't want to continue?"
  • "Do you ever use punishment or restraint?"
  • "Will you try to stop my child's stimming?"
  • "How many hours a day is my child at a table?"
  • "Can I watch a session?"
  • "How are your therapists trained and supervised?"

A good center will welcome every one of these questions. If a center avoids them, keep looking.
